# The Harbor Town

The harbor town woke slowly on the first cold morning of autumn. Fishing boats rocked against their moorings while gulls argued over scraps along the quay.

A baker on the corner street had been awake for hours. She shaped 40 loaves before sunrise, dusted each one with flour, and slid them into the oven in careful rows. By 7:30 the smell of fresh bread had drifted down to the water, and people followed it without thinking, the way you follow a familiar song.

## An Old Customer

"Good morning, Mrs. Alder," said an old man in a blue cap. "The usual, please."

He bought the same thing every day: one round loaf, still warm, and a small paper bag of raisin buns for his granddaughter. It cost him $4.50, which he counted out in coins. He liked to say that a town is only as good as its bread and its gossip [3], and that this town was lucky enough to have plenty of both.
